When you hit 11th St, look east to see a tall metal structure in the distance with a ring of lights on top. It’s one of Austin’s 17 remaining moonlight towers - 165-foot-tall structures erected in the late 19th century to provide artificial moonlight.

Thirty-one of these metal giants were erected in 1895 using electricity from Austin's first power plant on the Colorado River. Originally, a worker took an elevator to the top of each one every night to light the carbon arc lamps. These were replaced in the 1920s by incandescent lamps and then by mercury vapor lamps in 1936 that were controlled by a switch.
